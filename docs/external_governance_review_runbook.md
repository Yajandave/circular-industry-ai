# Circular Industry AI — external governance review runbook

## Purpose

This runbook is for a professional review conversation about Circular Industry AI's governance architecture.

The goal is **not** to seek endorsement or demonstrate every feature. The goal is to expose the controlled decision workflow, invite challenge and identify where governance is still weak.

## Product position

Circular Industry AI is a controlled circular-economy decision-support alpha for industrial material and waste streams.

It:
- profiles operational data
- applies deterministic circular-economy screening rules
- identifies risk and evidence gaps
- routes selected cases to human review
- develops supplier/procurement and implementation actions
- screens intervention scenarios
- records observed outcomes
- applies internal evidence and claim-readiness controls
- uses optional AI for explanation and drafting without overriding governance-critical fields

It is **not**:
- legal advice or regulatory approval
- independent assurance
- a verified savings/diversion/carbon system
- a complete ESG, GHG, EIA or green-claims engine
- an autonomous decision maker

## Recommended 30-minute flow

### 0–3 minutes — problem and design principle

Explain the original question:

> How can operational waste and material-flow data inform better circular-economy decisions without simply becoming another sustainability report?

Then explain the governing principle:

> AI should support the decision process, not become the silent decision maker.

### 3–9 minutes — one rules-cleared case

Show one straightforward material stream.

Demonstrate:
1. source operational data
2. deterministic rule selected
3. recommended circular route
4. qualitative evidence maturity
5. decision-support band
6. missing evidence
7. supplier/operational next action
8. rule provenance

Do not describe the output as a verified recommendation.

### 9–15 minutes — one review-gated case

Show a hazardous, classification-sensitive or otherwise high-risk stream.

Demonstrate:
1. the system refuses routine optimisation
2. human review is required
3. reviewer competence is routed explicitly
4. a second review may be recommended
5. public guidance informs the safety/classification boundary
6. the software states that this is not legal classification or assurance

This contrast is more important than showing many records.

### 15–20 minutes — scenario, outcome and claim boundary

Show the distinction between:
- screened intervention scenario
- observed outcome
- internally reviewed factual statement
- external verification

Explain that:
- scenario quantities are modelled, not achieved
- observed outcomes are evidence-linked but not automatically claim-ready
- documentary evidence is not the same as independent assurance
- carbon, financial savings, legal compliance and verified diversion remain blocked without separate evidence/verification

### 20–24 minutes — disagreement governance

Open the controlled review pack.

Show:
- rule provenance
- required reviewer competence
- no-silent-override policy
- "record professional disagreement"

Explain:
- a reviewer can challenge route, risk, review gate, evidence or claim boundary
- the original rules-engine recommendation remains historically intact
- the challenge is stored as a separate governance record
- the alpha does not yet have authenticated override authority

### 24–27 minutes — blind external review

Explain the blind-review method:
1. reviewer receives case only
2. system recommendation remains hidden
3. reviewer selects strategy, risk and human-review requirement
4. reviewer gives confidence and reasoning
5. submission is stored
6. comparison appears only after submission
7. disagreements are retained
8. consensus is not treated as ground truth

Do not present internal benchmark or grounded challenge agreement as real-world accuracy.

### 27–30 minutes — expert challenge questions

Ask:

1. What would make you uncomfortable about trusting this architecture?
2. Are the boundaries between deterministic rules, AI assistance and human judgement clear enough?
3. Is the evidence/claim-readiness model too permissive, too restrictive or missing a stage?
4. Is the reviewer-competence routing sensible, and when would you require a second reviewer?
5. How should an organisation govern overrides when a qualified professional disagrees with the system?
6. What rule/source provenance would you expect before relying on a recommendation?
7. Is blind review a credible first external challenge method, and what would you change?
8. What would you want to see before this could move beyond an internal alpha?

## Demo safety checks before the call

- backend health endpoint returns OK
- frontend connects to local backend
- sample dataset loads
- recommendation run completes
- review pack opens for both chosen cases
- provenance links render
- scenario screen works
- observed-outcome / claim-readiness history loads
- blind-review portal loads without system answers
- review-analysis page does not claim consensus from one reviewer
- optional AI path works if it will be shown
- browser zoom and screen share are readable
- no fake local reviewer records are presented as external validation

## Claims to avoid

Do not say:
- validated AI
- independently verified
- 100% accurate
- regulator-approved
- verified savings
- verified diversion
- autonomous sustainability decision maker
- full ESG/GHG/EIA/green-claims capability

Safer wording:
- controlled decision-support alpha
- deterministic screening logic
- authoritative-guidance challenge suite
- internal benchmark
- blind external-review workflow
- evidence and claim-readiness controls
- AI-assisted explanation and drafting
- prepared for external challenge

## Desired meeting outcome

The most valuable outcome is not endorsement.

The most valuable outcome is a documented list of:
- governance weaknesses
- missing controls
- evidence concerns
- override/approval expectations
- reviewer-competence concerns
- validation-method improvements
- conditions that would increase professional trust
